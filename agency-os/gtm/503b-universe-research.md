## (b) Ranked new Tier-1 candidate shortlist

Fit test applied: small-to-midsize · office-use to clinics/practices (not primarily hospital GPO) · not GLP-1-dependent · no in-house national sales force · no FDA warning letter in last ~18 months (i.e., nothing since ~Jan 2025).

### Tier 1 — pursue

**1. McGuff Outsourcing Solutions (Santa Ana, CA)** — *best single fit*
Explicitly and publicly targets "physician practices, IV hydration clinics, and wellness/functional medicine providers" — the exact office-use channel, with hospitals a secondary mention. Zero GLP-1 exposure; the halo product is ASCOR, the only FDA-approved ascorbic acid injection, which is a defensible non-GLP-1 franchise. Just secured CA 503B licensure and is in the "building reliable inventory / expanding access" phase, i.e. pre-scale and likely under-distributed. No warning letter found. Family/closely-held McGuff Companies. Only caveat: the 503B arm is young, so revenue base may be thin.

**2. Molecular PharmaGroup (New Providence, NJ)**
Sells to "hospitals, medical groups and independent practitioners" — practitioner channel is named explicitly, not an afterthought. Newly constructed single site, small, no evidence of a national sales force. No GLP-1 signal. Outsources sterility/endotoxin/potency testing to a third-party QA firm, which is a cost structure a buyer could in-source. 483 issued 8/11/2025 but **no warning letter** — passes the test as written, though diligence should read that 483.

**3. Apocus, Inc. (San Antonio, TX)**
Positions on shortage/backordered/discontinued drugs plus clinical-trial and investigational supply — a differentiated, non-GLP-1, non-commodity niche. Sole outsourcing facility in San Antonio. Sterile and non-sterile. No warning letter or recall found. Small. Channel mix is the open question — verify hospital-vs-clinic split before advancing.

**4. Excite Pharma Services (Tonganoxie, KS)**
503B license granted 1/23/2025 on top of a 15-year CDMO base — meaning GMP infrastructure and staff already exist and the 503B is incremental capacity, an unusually clean asset profile. Product set (injectables, nasal gel, eye drops) is deliberately non-GLP-1. States it serves "pharmacies, doctors, and healthcare systems." No warning letter. Small, Kansas-based, founder/operator-scale.

**5. Epicur Pharma / Stokes Healthcare (Mount Laurel, NJ)**
Veterinary office-use is a structurally attractive adjacency: clinics buy office stock, there is no GPO stranglehold, no GLP-1 exposure, and Epicur claims the largest 503B animal-health catalog. Family-owned, two-division structure. The disqualifier risk is timing, not substance: warning letter 4/2/2024 sits outside the 18-month window but inside 24 months — treat as a compliance-remediation diligence item, not a pass/fail.

### Tier 2 — conditional

**6. Eagle Pharma Outsourcing (Hoover, AL)** — practitioner-channel fit is excellent (medical professionals in 28 states, personalized formulations, no GLP-1 emphasis) and there is no recent warning letter. **But it was acquired by Revelation Pharma in July 2023**, so it is inside a PE-backed rollup and unlikely to be independently available.

**7. SKNV (Pompano Beach / Fort Lauderdale, FL)** — dermatology office-use through provider EMR is a strong, sticky, non-GLP-1 channel. Two negatives: "4,000+ providers" and a 37,500 sq ft / +100-job expansion imply both scale and a real commercial organization, cutting against "small-to-midsize, no national sales force"; and the compliance arc is adverse — 483 (5/2024), 483 (3/2025), Untitled Letter (9/16/2025). An untitled letter is not a warning letter so it technically passes, but the trend is the wrong direction.

**8. Vitae Enim Vitae Scientific, ARC Compounding, PGRrx, World Reach Pharma, XSeer, Vivasor** — all small, all clean on enforcement, all **information-poor**. These are the highest-value diligence targets precisely because nothing is published about them; they are where an unworked, un-intermediated asset is most likely to sit. Recommend a primary-research pass (state board filings, LinkedIn headcount, D&B) before ranking.

## (c) Roster-completeness assessment

| Measure | Count |
|---|---|
| FDA-stated universe | **~93 registrations** (93 as of 7/16/2025; FDA table last updated 4/2/2026) |
| Previously known (prior research) | ~37 companies |
| Newly identified here | **42 additional named entities/sites** (Table a) |
| Additional registration slots confirmed for known companies | ~10 (QuVa ×2, Fagron ×2, Wells ×2, STAQ ×2, OSRX ×2, Anazao ×2, Empower ×2, Belmar/BSO ×2, plus Wedgewood ×2 counted as new) |
| **Distinct registrations I can now name with medium-or-better confidence** | **~78–82 of ~93 (≈85%)** |
| Named but 503B status **UNVERIFIED** | 5 (Pharmcore, Revive Rx, Meds For Vets, Allaymeds, Park Avenue Compounding) |
| Estimated unaccounted-for remainder | **~11–15 registrations** |

**Where the residual gap almost certainly sits.** Two structural reasons, not random omission:

1. **CAPS / B. Braun multi-site registrations.** CAPS operates roughly nine to eleven separately-registered geographic sites (Allentown PA, Phoenix AZ, and others each hold their own FDA row). The prior research counted "CAPS/B.Braun" as one name; on the FDA table it is closer to ten rows. This alone plausibly explains most of the residual.
2. **Recently-registered, never-inspected facilities with no digital footprint.** PSM found 39 of 48 facilities registered since June 2021 had never been inspected. Never-inspected facilities generate no 483s, no warning letters, and no FDA inspection-record URLs — which were my two most productive enumeration channels. They are therefore systematically invisible to this method. Xenith Pharmaceutical, Allaymeds, Park Avenue Compounding, Ajenat, and 503B Outsourcing Facility One are the ones I caught; there are likely ~8–10 more I did not.

**Geographic cross-check** (corroborates but does not prove completeness): facilities are registered across 27 states, concentrated in Florida, Texas, New Jersey, and California; Texas alone reportedly holds 15. My reconstruction yields 14–15 Texas entries, consistent.

**Recommendation to close the gap to 100%:** the FDA table is the only authoritative source and requires either (a) an egress-policy exception for `www.fda.gov` for this workspace, or (b) retrieval from an unblocked network. Two lower-friction substitutes that would each close most of the remainder: FDA's **6-month product reports** (which list every registrant plus its actual product catalog — far better for the "product focus" and "GLP-1-dependent" columns than anything used here), and a **state nonresident-503B permit registry pull** from 3–4 large states (SC, NV, MN, and MA all publish nonresident outsourcing permit lists), which would catch the never-inspected facilities that FDA enforcement records structurally miss.
