## 60-second business description (open with this, verbatim-ready)

"I run Sonoran Clinical Partners, a marketing and introduction agency for 503B outsourcing facilities. I find independent clinical buyers — men's health clinics, IV therapy, med spas with medical directors — verify their licensure at the primary source, and make warm introductions. The facility is seller-of-record on everything: they quote, price, invoice, ship. I never take title or possession, never transmit pricing, never negotiate a sale. Compensation is pay-on-performance — a fee per qualified meeting held plus a commission on collected revenue, with a contractual exclusion of anything billed to a federal healthcare program. I'm signing my first facilities now and I want the structure confirmed before I scale it."

## Questions, in priority order

1. **The licensing line.** Does the introduction model as described stay outside "wholesale distribution"/broker licensing in your experience — and which states would you worry about? (Mention: guidance letters already out to AZ, OH, TN boards. Ask if that was smart or if written board inquiries create a record they'd have advised against.)
2. **Controlled substances + men's health.** I'm narrowing toward TRT/men's health clinics. Testosterone is Schedule III. What does that change — office-use CS rules state-by-state, the Georgia Schedule II–V office-use exclusion, anything federal? **This is now the highest-stakes question given the niche.** Can a facility like Belmar lawfully ship office-use testosterone to clinics in which states?
3. **Compensation structure.** Per-qualified-meeting fees + % of collected revenue (pay-on-performance, no retainer) with a federal-program exclusion clause — does that hold up under AKS and the state all-payor statutes? Anything they'd change in the structure itself?
4. **Florida.** We carve out FL buyers entirely because of the Patient Brokering Act's "patronage" language. Overkill or correct?
5. **The CA/AL/NJ question.** Our research suggests the common "office-use prohibited" flags for those states are 503A rules misapplied to 503Bs, with CA actually being a supplier-licensure gate post-SB 1193. Is that read right?
6. **Supplier-side red flags.** If a facility has a recent warning letter or recall (we're talking to one), what's the agency's exposure in introducing buyers to it — and what diligence would they consider adequate?
7. **Engagement scope + cost.** What would it cost to: review the supplier agreement template, sanity-check the outreach/claims rules, and be on call for state-specific questions? Fixed-fee options?

## What NOT to do on this call

- Don't name ProRx or any specific facility's regulatory history — keep question 6 hypothetical.
- Don't ask them to bless anything ("so we're fine, right?") — they can't pre-engagement, and pushing wastes the goodwill.
- Don't commit to an engagement on the call. Get the quote, decide after.
