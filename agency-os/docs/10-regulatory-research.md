# Regulatory Research & Lowest-Barrier Model Selection

Research date: 2026-07-27. Synthesized from five parallel deep-research reports (state licensing/DSCSA, FDA product rules, compensation law, outreach law, comparable businesses) with primary-source citations throughout. This is research to navigate by, not legal advice; items the sources could not confirm are marked UNVERIFIED. Full source URL lists are preserved at the end of each section.

---

## 1. The decision: run a Marketing & Introduction Agency, not a sales broker

The single most important finding across all five reports: **regulatory exposure is determined by activities, not by what you call yourself.** The licensing and enforcement lines are:

| Activity | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Taking title or possession of drugs | DSCSA trading partner + state wholesale distributor licensing everywhere. **Never do this.** |
| **Negotiating, offering, or contracting for the sale** of drugs — even with zero possession | Captured by broker/virtual-wholesaler definitions in CA ("negotiates for distribution"), TX ("offering or contracting for wholesale distribution"), FL (broker permit), AZ (virtual wholesaler), IL (brokers listed). FDA's own test (2022 proposed rule): ownership **or** "authority to direct the sale or disposition." |
| **Introducing and marketing** — supplier is seller-of-record, sends its own quotes, sets its own prices, invoices directly | Outside "wholesale distribution" on the face of the federal definitions (no distribution or receipt of drug, no ownership transfer, not a "trading partner" under FD&C §581(23)-(24)). This is how the existing industry of 1099 rep networks operates — no distributor licenses held. |
| In-person selling to health professionals in certain places | Rep-licensing triggers: DC (in-person detailing), Chicago (>15 days/yr in person), Oregon (15+ days/yr), Nevada (registry, 5+ days physical presence). **All are in-person/physical-presence triggers — remote email/phone outreach avoids every one of them.** |

**Model chosen (launch mode): remote-only Marketing & Introduction Agency.**
- You find and qualify licensed buyers, run compliant cold outreach, and make warm introductions to suppliers.
- The supplier quotes, negotiates, contracts, invoices, ships, and is seller-of-record for everything.
- You never transmit pricing to buyers, never take an order, never "direct the sale or disposition."
- All selling is remote (email/phone/video) — no rep-license jurisdictions triggered.
- Result: **zero licenses required to start**, on the research above. The barrier to entry is an LLC, a domain, and discipline about which activities you don't do.

The original full-broker design (agency composes and sends quotes — workflow WF13/gate G6) is the one piece that crosses the "offering for sale" line. It is deferred, not deleted: see §6 migration path.

## 2. Revenue structure — ranked by speed and cleanliness

| Structure | Legal character | Speed to cash | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Monthly retainer + per-qualified-meeting fee** | Pure marketing services; no tie to drug sales volume at all | **Fastest** — paid from month one, close or no close | Cleanest possible: nothing for any kickback or broker statute to grab. Standard lead-gen pricing. |
| **2. Percentage commission on supplier's collected sales** | Lawful in cash-pay B2B per the compensation research: federal AKS unmet when no federal-program billing (its own elements); EKRA doesn't cover pharmacies/drug suppliers; OIG Advisory Opinion 98-10 blessed a B2B commission agent to institutions; *U.S. v. Sorensen* (7th Cir. 2025) and *Marchetti* (5th Cir. 2024) — percentage pay to marketers without clinical-decision influence is not a kickback | Slower (paid on collections) but scales with account value | Requires the contract terms in §5. Industry norm 10–30% for independent med-product reps. |
| 3. Commission + agency-run quoting/negotiation | Crosses into broker territory in the red-flag states | — | Deferred (see §6). |

**Recommendation: start hybrid — retainer + per-meeting fee for immediate revenue, with a commission schedule in the same contract that activates on closed accounts.** Suppliers accept this readily (it's cheaper for them than a full commission-only rep until you prove volume), and it front-loads your cash flow.

## 3. Product lanes — ranked

From the FDA research (2025–2026 enforcement posture):

| Lane | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-GLP-1 office-use compounded preparations from 503B outsourcing facilities** (e.g., sterile injectables clinics use in-office: numbing, IV nutrients, hormone preparations, urology/derm/ophtho compounds) | **PRIMARY LANE** | Legal channel: 503Bs may sell non-patient-specific "office use" directly to providers. Conditions: 503B must hold the destination state's permit (they manage this; you verify), avoid office-use-restricted states (CA, AL, NJ flagged; full list UNVERIFIED — check per state), copy discipline per §4. |
| FDA-approved generics via licensed wholesalers; medical devices; compliant supplements | GREEN but crowded | Lowest product risk; approved-drug brokering re-raises the wholesale-licensing question (DSCSA chain), so as an *introducer* only. Devices/supplements have no drug-licensing issue at all — good fillers. |
| 503A patient-specific compounded drugs | Situational | Patient-specific prescriptions only, never clinic office-stock (federal position). Narrower use for a B2B agency. |
| **Compounded GLP-1s (semaglutide/tirzepatide/liraglutide)** | **DO NOT TOUCH** | Shortage exception ended 2025; FDA moving to bar permanently (April 2026 proposed bulks-list exclusion); ~130+ warning letters in 2025–26 **including to marketing intermediaries**; Novo/Lilly suing marketers; 40+ injunctions. The market that looks biggest is the one being actively dismantled. |
| **"Research use only" peptides toward med spas/clinics** | **DO NOT TOUCH** | RUO disclaimer is legally void when human use is foreseeable (21 CFR 201.128 intended-use test); continuous FDA warning-letter waves Dec 2024 → April 2026. Selling to genuine research institutions is the only defensible RUO lane and it's a poor agency business. |

This is the honest version of "plenty of businesses do exactly this": the ones doing **non-GLP-1 503B office-use** and device/med-product rep work operate openly and unlicensed as 1099 marketing agents. The ones doing GLP-1s and "research peptides" are the ones getting the warning letters, AG actions, and manufacturer lawsuits. Same middleman shape — the product choice is the risk choice.

## 4. Outreach — confirmed lawful, with a short hard-rule list

Cold B2B email to pharmacies/clinics/hospitals is **lawful** (CAN-SPAM is opt-out; no consent needed; no B2B exemption but no B2B restriction either). The system already implements the mechanics; the non-negotiables:

1. Truthful sender identity + real postal address + working one-click opt-out; honor within 10 business days (we do it instantly). Penalties: up to ~$53k/email for violations; California §17529.5 adds $1,000/email private suits for deceptive headers/subjects — truthful "From" and subject lines are law, not style.
2. **Content is where enforcement lives:** never "FDA-approved," "generic version of," "same as [brand]," or any safety/efficacy claim for compounded preparations; identify products as compounded; factual sourced statements only. The 2025–26 warning-letter waves hit *marketers* on exactly these phrases.
3. Calls: live manual dialing to business lines is fine. **No automated/prerecorded/AI-voice calls or texts to cell numbers without prior express written consent** (TCPA, $500–$1,500 per violation, active class-action bar). Cold texting via automation is the trap to avoid.
4. Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (SPF/DKIM/DMARC, one-click unsub honored in 2 days, complaint rate <0.3%) are operational requirements to reach inboxes at all.
5. CCPA applies to B2B contact data only if you exceed thresholds ($25M revenue or 100k CA consumers' records/yr) — track CA record counts as list-buying scales.

## 5. Compensation contract terms (from the case law and safe-harbor factors)

Put these in every supplier agreement; they are what makes the commission clean:

1. **Federal-program exclusion:** no commission accrues on any product billed or billable to Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, VA, or any federal/state program; supplier certifies, audit right, automatic carve-out. (This single clause removes the federal AKS hook entirely.)
2. **Scope limits:** agency markets to licensed institutional purchasers only — no patient contact, no prescriber inducement, no influence over clinical or patient-specific decisions, no payments to buyer personnel. (The exact factors from OIG AO 98-10 and *Sorensen*.)
3. **Safe-harbor styling:** written, ≥1-year term, services specified, commission methodology fixed in advance, rate in the documented 10–30% industry norm.
4. **Entity exclusions:** no commissionable business involving labs, recovery homes, or clinical treatment facilities (EKRA is all-payor for those and bans commissions even to employees).
5. **Seller-of-record clause:** supplier quotes, prices, contracts, invoices, ships; agency never takes title/possession or directs sale/disposition (this is the licensing shield from §1, in writing).

## 7. Launch-mode changes to the system (applied)

The Phase-1 system built earlier stays almost entirely intact — buyers still get verified, gates still run, everything still audits. Three changes align it to the introduction model:

1. **WF13 (quotes):** the agency no longer composes or transmits quotes. The supplier sends quotes directly to the buyer; the agency *tracks* quote status as supplier-reported data (same as orders). Gate G6 becomes a data-quality check (supplier-reported quote must reference an introduced opportunity), not a send-gate.
2. **Decision register:** the twelve "requires counsel" blockers are replaced by a research-based navigation register (see `00-decision-table.md` §4, rewritten). Founder clears each item by recording the decision with this document as basis. The honest footnote: a healthcare-regulatory attorney reviewing the supplier contract template and first campaign copy is a ~$2–4k one-time cost that buys a lot of certainty on the two genuinely untested points (FL PBA scope, board reads on "introduction ≠ wholesale distribution") — it's noted, priced, and left as the founder's call, not a gate.
3. **Product scope:** GLP-1 compounds and RUO peptides are seeded as prohibited categories (no `product_approved_buyer_types` / no geo-allow rows can be created for them without founder override + basis).

## 8. Launch checklist — fastest compliant path to revenue

1. Form the LLC (AZ), EIN, bank account, general liability + E&O quote. (Week 1)
2. Buy brand domain + 2 sending-domain variants; set up SPF/DKIM/DMARC, warm-up. (Week 1, parallel)
3. Stand up the Airtable MVP per `07-stack.md` / `08-build-plan.md`. (Week 1–2)
4. Sign 1–2 suppliers on the §5 contract terms: retainer + per-meeting fee + commission schedule, seller-of-record clause, federal-program exclusion. Target non-GLP-1 503Bs (find via FDA's registered outsourcing-facility list; APC/OFA member lists). (Week 2–3)
5. Verify each supplier's state licenses for your target states (their permits, not yours — this is your diligence product, and it's a selling point to buyers).
6. Import buyer prospects (clinics/practices in target specialties, excluding FL/CA/AL/NJ for office-use products); qualify; verify licenses via state board portals; launch approved sequences. (Week 3–4)
7. First introductions → supplier quotes directly → track reported orders → invoice retainer/meeting fees monthly, commission on collections.
8. As volume concentrates in a watch-tier state, get the board determination or permit then — not before.

Estimated startup cost: LLC + insurance ~$800–1,500; software ~$180–260/mo (per `07-stack.md`); no licensing spend at launch.
